📏 Med Math Conversion Reference (study before you start)
1 unit = 1,000 milliunits (mU)
1 g = 1,000 mg
1 mg = 1,000 mcg
1 L = 1,000 mL
1 kg = 2.2 lb
1 lb = 16 oz = 454 g
1 oz = 30 mL
1 hr = 60 min
QBL: 1 g = 1 mL blood
Formula: 20 kcal/oz (standard)
Newborn needs: 100–120 kcal/kg/day
Diapers: 1 g = 1 mL urine
Moving the decimal: big unit → small unit, multiply (g → mg: ×1,000). Small unit → big unit, divide (mcg → mg: ÷1,000).
Oxytocin: concentration (mU/mL) = units × 1,000 ÷ bag mL · mL/hr = mU/min × 60 ÷ mU/mL · mU/min = mL/hr × mU/mL ÷ 60.
Weight-based infusions: g/hr ÷ (g ÷ mL in the bag) = mL/hr (magnesium) · units/kg/hr × kg = units/hr, then ÷ units/mL = mL/hr (heparin).
Drip factor (gravity): gtt/min = (total mL × drop factor) ÷ total minutes · or (mL/hr × drop factor) ÷ 60. Macrodrip = 10, 15 or 20 gtt/mL; microdrip = 60 gtt/mL.
Other formulas: Dose (mL) = desired ÷ have × volume · Timed infusion mL/hr = mL ÷ minutes × 60 · QBL = wet g − dry g (add measured mL without weighing it twice) · % weight loss = (birth − current) ÷ birth × 100.
Solving word problems: 1) Find what you're solving for (mL? gtt/min? mL/hr? mU/min?). 2) Circle only the order, weight and concentration on hand. 3) Convert units FIRST (units→mU, lb/oz→kg, mg↔mcg, min↔hr). 4) Round only at the end (tenths for mL unless told otherwise, hundredths for small newborn IM doses, whole numbers for gtt/min). 5) Ask: does this answer make sense for this client or newborn?
